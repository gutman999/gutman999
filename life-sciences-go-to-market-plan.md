## The Opportunity: Why Life Sciences, Why Now

Software now powers every critical function in life sciences, from research informatics and bioinformatics pipelines to regulated device development and clinical operations. Engineering velocity, code quality, and AI governance are no longer nice-to-haves. They are strategic priorities with board-level attention.

Three forces are converging to make this the right moment.

**Developer velocity is a competitive moat.** Drug discovery timelines, clinical software release cycles, and medical device iteration speeds are increasingly determined by how fast engineering teams can move. AI coding tools are becoming as foundational as cloud infrastructure.

**Governance is creating a wedge.** Enterprise security and compliance teams are starting to audit AI tooling across regulated environments. Companies still running unsanctioned or fragmented AI coding tools are about to face pressure to standardize. That pressure is a sales trigger.

**Cursor already has adoption.** The hardest part, generating developer demand, is already done. The sales motion here is not education from zero. It is identifying where usage is spreading and helping organizations scale it with the controls they need to formalize it.

## Competitive Positioning

Life sciences enterprise evaluations are primarily won or lost on security posture, governance confidence, and measurable impact, not features.

| Evaluation Criterion | Cursor | GitHub Copilot | Windsurf / Codeium |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Privacy and compliance** | Enterprise-grade controls with policy-oriented workflows built for regulated environments | Strong enterprise trust via the GitHub and Microsoft ecosystem, often evaluated as part of broader platform governance | Improving posture; requires additional diligence with risk and legal teams in conservative accounts |
| **Agentic capabilities** | AI-native, agent-forward experience built for complex iterative tasks and workflow acceleration | Broad assistant adoption; generally perceived as less agent-centric in daily workflows | Agentic features evolving quickly; compelling in technical evaluations |
| **Enterprise admin controls** | Built for IT and security governance conversations around rollout control, team standards, and policy enforcement | Mature admin pathways for enterprises already standardized on GitHub Enterprise | Available but often needs additional validation for large, controlled rollouts |
| **Model flexibility** | Flexible model strategy for teams balancing performance, cost, and policy requirements | Tight GitHub ecosystem integration with less perceived flexibility | Competitive model options with experimentation appeal; long-term governance proof may be requested |
| **Adoption and ROI** | Clear story around development velocity, throughput, and repeatable productivity gains | High awareness and easy initial adoption; ROI tied to existing platform usage patterns | Can show fast pilot momentum; enterprise-scale ROI may require stronger multi-team evidence |

Lead with business impact and regulated-enterprise fit. Differentiate on agentic workflow quality and deployment confidence. Never lead with features.

## Key Discovery Questions

**For the VP Engineering:** Which software delivery bottlenecks are currently slowing your ability to ship critical R&D and digital initiatives?

**For the Head of R&D IT:** Where do your teams spend the most time in repetitive coding, review, or documentation workflows that could be accelerated safely?

**For the Chief Digital Officer:** What strategic outcomes matter most over the next 12 months, faster innovation, lower development cost, or better cross-team execution, and how are those currently measured?

**For Security and Compliance leaders:** What controls must any AI coding platform meet to satisfy GxP and FDA expectations around auditability and enterprise governance?

**For Platform and Architecture teams:** How are you standardizing developer tooling across business units today, and where are fragmentation or adoption gaps creating consistency problems?

**For Bioinformatics and Data Science leaders:** Which high-impact pipelines or internal tools would benefit most from faster iteration without compromising quality or reproducibility?

**For Procurement and Finance:** What ROI threshold and business case structure are required to approve a new developer productivity platform at enterprise scale?

**For the broader buying committee:** If a pilot succeeds technically, what non-technical blockers typically delay rollout across additional teams and geographies?

## GTM Motion: Converting PLG Adoption into Enterprise Scale

### What Is Happening

Cursor is experiencing one of the fastest bottom-up adoption curves in modern software. Developers are finding the product independently and generating immediate value. Usage is spreading across teams without a formal buying process, and many companies are already benefiting from Cursor before leadership is fully aware of how widely it is being used.

This is strong product-led growth. The challenge is that adoption is now moving faster than most enterprises can organize around it.

### The Risk

Rapid organic adoption creates predictable enterprise tension. Security and compliance teams begin asking about code exposure and data governance. Teams use the product inconsistently without centralized visibility. Procurement is not yet involved, which delays monetization and creates contractual risk. And without structure, a competitor can attempt to enterprise-ize what Cursor already started, positioning themselves as the governance layer on top of existing usage.

### The Opportunity

The hardest part is already done: developers want the product and are actively using it. This creates a fundamentally different sales motion, one focused on identifying where usage is strong and helping organizations scale it intentionally rather than generating demand from zero.

Enterprises will pay for security, governance, standardization, and measurable productivity gains. The conversion path runs from organic individual usage to sanctioned team adoption to enterprise standard.

### Guiding Principles

**Product-led growth stays the engine.** Sales supports and accelerates what is already happening. It does not impose process on top of it.

**Engagement is signal-driven, not pipeline-driven.** Conversations are initiated based on usage spread and governance trigger events, not quota pressure.

**Developer experience is non-negotiable.** Any enterprise motion that degrades the developer experience destroys the adoption that created the opportunity.

## Risks and Mitigations

**Long procurement cycles delay revenue conversion.** Life sciences enterprise deals commonly run 9 to 18 months due to security reviews, legal diligence, and multi-stakeholder consensus. This creates early-quarter pipeline drag.

Mitigation: Prioritize accounts with existing Cursor usage signals to shorten the path from discovery to commercial. Where usage is already present, the technical risk conversation is partially resolved. The remaining work is governance and procurement, which can move in parallel with an active pilot.

**Security and compliance requirements create qualification drop-off.** Some accounts will have GxP or FDA constraints that require extended security review before any enterprise agreement is possible.

Mitigation: Build a compliance qualification gate early in the discovery process. Prioritize accounts where Cursor's existing enterprise controls already satisfy the majority of requirements and deprioritize accounts requiring extensive custom compliance work until product capability catches up.

**Organic developer adoption gets disrupted before it converts.** If a CISO or compliance team issues a blanket AI tooling ban before enterprise conversations are initiated, usage could be frozen or rolled back, eliminating the adoption signal that created the opportunity.

Mitigation: Treat governance trigger events, including security reviews, CISO audits, and procurement inquiries, as top-priority engagement signals. The goal is to be in the room before the ban conversation starts, positioning Cursor as the compliant path forward rather than the product being evaluated for risk.
